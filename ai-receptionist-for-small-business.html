What is an AI receptionist?

An AI receptionist is software that handles incoming business calls and common customer interactions. Depending on the product, it can answer questions, collect caller information, route calls, schedule appointments, send messages and provide summaries after calls.

The important distinction is that “AI receptionist” is not one standardized product category. Some services are primarily call answering; others sit inside a larger business phone system.

What should a small business look for?

Call answering and call handling

The basic job is simple: answer calls consistently and handle routine conversations without forcing every caller to voicemail.

Appointment booking

For appointment-based businesses, scheduling can be more valuable than generic conversational ability. Check which calendars or booking systems are supported.

After-hours and overflow calls

An AI receptionist can be useful outside normal business hours or when staff are already busy. Confirm exactly how after-hours routing works.

Human call transfer

Good systems should have a clear escalation path when a caller needs a person. Transfer behavior and availability should be checked before choosing a service.

Pricing and usage limits

Compare the actual unit of billing: calls, minutes, credits or AI interactions. A low headline price can mean very different things depending on usage.

Best AI receptionists for a small business

1. Upfirst — Best overall for most small businesses

Upfirst is a straightforward fit when the goal is dedicated AI-assisted call answering without building a broader communications stack. Its published plans include 24/7 answering, call summaries, recordings/transcription, warm transfers and appointment scheduling. The Starter plan is listed at $24.95/month for 30 calls, with higher tiers available.

Why it stands out: the core receptionist workflow is easy to understand, with useful options for businesses that mainly want calls answered and routed.

Watch for: usage beyond the included allowance can change the economics, so compare expected call volume with the plan limits.

2. Quo Sona — Best for usage-based answering

Sona is Quo's AI receptionist. Its pricing uses credits and call-based usage, with plans ranging from a $0 tier to higher monthly allowances. Sona can answer calls, help with scheduling workflows, provide call summaries and support transfers.

Why it stands out: the usage model can be attractive when a business wants to match spending more closely to call volume.

Watch for: Sona is part of the Quo ecosystem, so the entry price should not be interpreted as a completely standalone free business-phone system.

3. Nextiva XBert — Best for broader business communications

XBert is Nextiva's AI receptionist offering. Its published product information includes AI call handling, text messaging, live chat, appointment booking, calendar/CRM integrations and smart handoff. The AI receptionist product is listed at $99/month for 100 AI interactions, with additional interactions priced separately.

Why it stands out: it makes the most sense when the business also values a wider communications platform rather than only an AI answering service.

Watch for: the starting price is higher than the simpler receptionist-first options, and the full platform context matters when comparing costs.

Which AI receptionist is best for different businesses?

  • Want a straightforward receptionist-first solution? Start by evaluating Upfirst.
  • Already using Quo? Sona deserves a closer look because it fits into that ecosystem.
  • Need broader voice, text and business communications? Nextiva XBert is the more platform-oriented option.
  • Need appointment handling? Compare the actual calendar integrations and booking workflow, not just whether “appointment scheduling” appears on the feature list.
  • Need after-hours coverage? Check the exact routing and escalation rules before subscribing.

Are AI receptionists worth it for a small business?

They can be, particularly when the cost of missed calls or slow responses is higher than the software cost. The strongest use cases are businesses with frequent inbound calls, appointment requests, repetitive questions or meaningful after-hours demand.

The right comparison is not simply “AI versus a receptionist.” It is whether the software reliably handles the specific calls that currently consume staff time or get missed.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an AI receptionist cost?

It varies by provider and billing model. The options compared here range from a low entry tier to $99/month for Nextiva XBert's published AI receptionist product. Always check current pricing and usage limits.

Can an AI receptionist book appointments?

Yes, the products compared here advertise appointment or scheduling capabilities, but the supported calendars and workflow differ.

Can an AI receptionist answer calls after hours?

Yes. After-hours coverage is a common use case, but businesses should verify the routing rules and escalation options.

Can an AI receptionist transfer calls to a human?

Yes. The products compared here support forms of transfer or handoff, though the exact workflow differs.

Can I keep my existing business number?

Some services support forwarding an existing number, while broader phone platforms may use different setup methods. Confirm the provider's current onboarding options.

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a human receptionist?

Software can cost less than employing a dedicated full-time receptionist, but the comparison should include what tasks the human performs and what the AI service actually handles.
